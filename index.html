Privacy Policy for Dock

Last updated: October 12, 2026

Dock is developed by Dekat ("we," "us"). This policy explains what information Dock collects, how it's used, and your choices — in plain terms, matching exactly what the app actually does today.

The short version

Everything Dock tracks — your paired tag, lock history, modes, schedules, and blocked-app lists — stays on your device. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else. There's no account, no sign-in, and no server Dock talks to.

What Dock stores, and where

All of the following is stored locally on your device only, in Dock's own private app storage:

  • Your paired NFC tag's ID — so Dock can recognize your tag on future taps.
  • Dock/undock history — timestamps and durations of each session, used to build the Activity tab's charts and averages.
  • Modes, schedules, and your blocked app/website selections — whatever you've configured.
  • Blocked notifications — if a notification arrives from a blocked app while you're docked, Dock records that app's name, the notification's title and text, and when it arrived, so you can see what you missed without unblocking the app. This is cleared automatically at the start of your next lock.

None of this is transmitted anywhere. Uninstalling Dock deletes all of it permanently.

Permissions Dock uses, and why

  • NFC — to read your physical tag and trigger a lock/unlock.
  • Accessibility Service — this is the permission that lets Dock actually block apps and enforce Strict Mode. Specifically, it reads the package name of whichever app is in the foreground, to check it against your blocked list, and for web browsers specifically, reads the address bar's URL to check it against your blocked websites list. It does not read message content, keystrokes, passwords, or any other on-screen text or content — only the foreground app's identity and, for browsers, the URL bar.
  • Notifications (post + listener access) — to detect and record notifications from blocked apps while you're docked (see "Blocked notifications" above), and to show Dock's own status notifications.
  • Schedule exact alarm — so scheduled locks/unlocks fire at the exact minute you set, rather than being delayed by the system.
  • Boot completed — to re-arm your schedules after your phone restarts.

What Dock does not do

  • No account or sign-in is required to use any current feature.
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising SDKs are included in the app today.
  • No data is sold or shared with third parties.
  • No data leaves your device.

Planned features (not yet live)

This policy will be updated before each of these ships, but in brief:

  • Ads — a future free-tier version of Dock may show ads served by Google AdMob. AdMob's own data practices will apply to ad delivery at that point, and this policy will be updated with specifics and a link to Google's own privacy policy.
  • In-app purchases — a future paid unlock and optional emergency-exit purchases will be processed entirely through Google Play Billing. Dock never sees or stores your payment details — Google handles that directly.
  • Optional Google Drive backup — a future, strictly opt-in setting (off by default) that would back up your settings, modes, and schedules — not your lock/activity history — to your own Google Drive, using Google's app-private storage scope that only Dock can read.

None of these exist in the app yet. If you're reading this and any of the above is live, this section will have been replaced with the real details.

Children's privacy

Dock is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date at the top will change, and the new version will be posted at this same page.
